Cover letter for an engineering internship: a four-part method that fits on one page

Updated 13 October 2026.

A recruiter reads a cover letter in under a minute. Most letters fail for the same reasons: they could be sent to any company, they list qualities without proof, and they repeat the CV. This method fixes all three, in four short parts that fit on one A4 page.

The four parts

  1. Why them. One precise reason for choosing this organization: a product, a project, a technology, recent news, something you saw at a forum. "A leader in its field" says nothing; "your switch to by-wire steering on the next platform" says you looked.
  2. What you bring, with proof. Two examples from your projects, internships or jobs, chosen to match what the posting asks for. For each: what you did, how, and the result, with a number when you can ("built a test bench that cut measurement time by a third"). Two solid examples beat six adjectives.
  3. Why this role. What you want to learn here and how it fits your studies or your plan. Show you understood the job: name one or two of its tasks in your own words.
  4. Closing. Your dates (start, duration, or that it's a required internship), an invitation to meet, and the closing formula.

The format

  • One A4 page, with a body of about 250 to 330 words, justified, in a plain font (11 to 12 pt).
  • Header: your name and contact details, the date, the recipient (a name if you found one, otherwise the team and the company, with its address), and a subject line with the posting's title and reference.
  • Signature under the closing: your name, and a scanned signature if you have one.
  • File: a PDF named Lastname_Firstname_CoverLetter_Company.pdf. Some forms ask for the letter and CV in a single PDF: read the instructions.

Write the letter in the posting's language, unless the posting asks otherwise.

Phrases to drop

  • "I am a dynamic, motivated and rigorous person": prove it with an example instead.
  • "Your company, a leader in its field": find a precise reason.
  • "As you can see in my CV": the letter should add what the CV can't say.
  • "I am passionate about…" without anything that shows it.

Before you send it

  • The company's name, the role and the reference are right (and not the previous company's).
  • It fits on one page, and within the form's character limit if you paste it into a field.
  • Your dates match what the posting asks for.
  • Someone else has read it, or you've read it aloud once.
